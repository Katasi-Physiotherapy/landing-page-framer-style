### Why a clinical physiotherapy assessment still matters after an X-ray or MRI

Have you had an X-ray or MRI for neck or back pain but still do not understand why you hurt or why treatment has not helped? 

Your scan is important, but it is only one part of the answer. [An X-ray](https://katasiphysiotherapy.co.ke/blog/x-ray-mri-back-pain-physiotherapy/) may show reduced disc spaces, spondylosis or changes in spinal alignment. An MRI may show a disc bulge, spinal stenosis or possible pressure around a nerve or the spinal cord. 

These findings can provide useful information, but they *do not always explain how your pain behaves or how it affects your daily life.* 

## What does a physiotherapist assess?

A physiotherapy assessment looks at the complete picture: your symptoms, posture, spinal and joint movement, muscle strength, flexibility, coordination, nerve-related signs, movement patterns and everyday activities. 

This helps a physiotherapist understand which factors may be contributing to your symptoms and whether physiotherapy or further medical review is appropriate. 

**Consider a comprehensive assessment** if pain is making it difficult to sit or stand comfortably, walk your usual distance, sleep through the night, work or exercise normally, or use your arms without pain, tingling or weakness. Unusual muscle fatigue is also worth discussing with a qualified physiotherapist.

## When should you seek prompt medical assessment?

Seek prompt medical assessment if you develop new or worsening difficulty walking, poor balance, weakness or numbness in your arms or legs, loss of hand coordination, or changes in bladder or bowel control. 

These symptoms **should not be ignored**. If you have tried several treatments without meaningful improvement or a clear explanation, do not lose hope. A thorough assessment may identify overlooked, treatable factors and clarify the next step.
